## Reference sites (what to borrow, what to skip)

- **jsomers.net** (James Somers, writer-programmer with New Yorker bylines). Almost no design at all: one column, list of essays by year, no images. Borrow: the confidence of a plain list; the essays-first hierarchy. Skip: total absence of visual identity.
- **maggieappleton.com**. Custom illustration, "digital garden" taxonomies, strong typographic scale. Borrow: how sections are labeled and how the intro paragraph is written in first person with links inline. Skip: the volume of custom art it demands.
- **colah.github.io** (Chris Olah). Research-first, Distill-style, figures as the hero. Borrow: figure captions as a design element (the `Fig. 1` caption on the homepage comes from here). Skip: dated layout.
- **craigmod.com**. Writer/photographer. Large serif, photography, newsletter integration front and center. Borrow: the newsletter-as-first-class-citizen treatment (the OTM section). Skip: heavy imagery.
- **gwern.net**. Dense, essay-centric, sidenotes, dropcaps, extreme typographic care. Borrow: seriousness about typography; hairline rules. Skip: density.
- **henrikkarlsson.xyz** (Escaping Flatland). Substack-native; shows what a writer's home looks like when Substack *is* the site. Useful as a contrast: this site is the hub, Substack is one spoke.
